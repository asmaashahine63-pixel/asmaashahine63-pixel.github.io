Project overview

Cooling a gas turbine blade through internal and external flow management

This project investigates how internal coolant passages and surface film-cooling holes affect the fluid flow and thermal behavior of a three-dimensional gas turbine blade. ANSYS Fluent was used to model the external hot-gas region and internal coolant passages. The study documents geometry preparation, meshing, solver setup, and post-processing of velocity, pressure, and temperature fields. A separate discussion considers enhanced cooling options including ribs, pin-fins, and angled film holes. The purpose of this portfolio page is to present the available report evidence accurately: original graphics are reproduced individually, while performance figures that lack full supporting comparative datasets are labeled as report claims.

Engineering interpretation and limitations

The report's post-processing images document the expected trade-off between cooling effectiveness and pressure loss: enhanced internal mixing can raise heat transfer while imposing more resistance, while film cooling can provide localized surface protection. Temperature and velocity gradients are clearly present in the provided contours, but the report does not include the full raw model, detailed solver convergence evidence, or a quantitative mesh-refinement study.

Numerical consistency notes: The text refers to blade temperatures approaching 2,000 K, while visible legends in some displayed thermal screenshots have maxima nearer 1,200–1,500 K. It also describes a temperature reduction to “680 degrees” without specifying units or a comparison baseline. These should remain attributed to the report rather than treated as verified extrema or demonstrated improvements. The reported node/element totals also merit checking against the original ANSYS mesh statistics.

The statements of 25–35% HTC improvement and 80–150 K temperature reduction are preserved as the report's claims, not independently reproduced values.
